## Verdict

**Strong revision. Not publication-ready.**

White-paper trap largely escaped. Concrete incident, named defendants, specific sentences, clear campaign endpoint now give readers something to grasp.

Core prescription: **architecture kept; concrete reporting recovered; overclaims reduced, not removed.**

Main remaining flaw: **separate facts still presented as one proven causal chain.** Household rabies incident, national surveillance figures, theft prosecution, public-opinion survey support different propositions. Copy sometimes makes them collectively prove more than supplied evidence establishes.

*Scope: assessment covers supplied copy and source placement. Linked reports, underlying dossier, survey methodology, petition destination remain unverified.*

## 1. Priority Refinements

### Incident-first opener: clear improvement

Previous opener—“Vietnam’s rabies figures demand focused action”—started with institutional interpretation. New opener starts with place, animal, four victims, positive test.

**Gain:** immediate stakes, geographic specificity, identifiable reporting.

“Rabies control cannot stop at household gates” works as headline and campaign thesis. Memorable without gore or cultural contempt.

**Remaining problem:** story leaves incident almost immediately. By sentence three, readers meet “untracked animal movement,” “improvised holding point,” “uninspected slaughter site.”

Concrete reporting becomes launchpad for abstraction rather than narrative spine.

**Fix:** give incident one more verified beat before widening scope: exposure circumstances, response, or treatment status—only if source supplies it. Otherwise move theft case earlier. Do not invent victim detail to manufacture drama.

### Crime reporting: recovered, still overextended

Named defendants, differing sentences, 19 intercepted dogs, reported cumulative tonnage supply specificity missing from Post B.

But:

> “They also expose a supply chain built on stolen companions and weak traceability.”

One prosecution can establish theft-linked supply activity **in that case**. It cannot establish that entire trade rests on stolen pets.

Better:

> “This case shows how stolen dogs entered one sales network. Its wider scale requires separate evidence.”

“Companions” also needs care. Ownership establishes theft; owners’ relationships with animals require reporting.

### Abolition endpoint: stronger advocacy, unresolved attribution

Closing sequence works:

> “Demand traceability now. Demand enforcement now. Demand abolition by 2030.”

Clear rhythm. Clear destination. No compromise hidden inside technical language.

But **campaign demand must remain distinct from official rabies policy**. Current wording risks borrowing state-policy authority for campaign’s preferred endpoint.

Better:

> “Vietnam’s rabies programme has its own public-health targets. Our campaign demands nationwide abolition of dog-meat slaughter, trade, and consumption by 2030.”

Verify official programme name, dates, scope, targets before publication.

## 2. Narrative Architecture, Tone & Pacing

### Heading 1: “One infected animal can cross many barriers”

**Strength:** links individual exposure with system-level prevention.

**Weakness:** “barriers” ambiguous. Household boundary? Provincial border? Veterinary safeguard? Heading also suggests infected animal moved through trade network. Supplied incident establishes no such movement.

Section then carries too much:

- Rabies transmission.
- Fatality.
- Emergency guidance.
- National statistics.
- Household-to-trade transition.
- Transport and slaughter risks.

**Fix:** split emergency guidance into short safety box. Trim statistical inventory. Name missing safeguards directly.

Possible heading:

> **Rabies prevention needs verified vaccination and safe handling**

### Heading 2: “Families reject black-market risk”

**Weakest section match.**

Heading promises family experience and rejection of illicit activity. Paragraph offers survey opposition to dog-meat trade generally, followed by court reporting.

Those subjects overlap politically, not evidentially. Opposition to dog meat does not automatically establish attitudes toward black-market biosecurity.

“Vietnamese families increasingly identify dogs as companions and protectors” asserts trend without longitudinal evidence. “Vietnamese people are demanding” turns respondents into national consensus.

**Fix:** lead section with theft case. Follow with properly scoped survey finding.

Possible heading:

> **Dog theft carries criminal consequences**

For stronger emotional resonance, add sourced owner testimony or documented loss. Names and tonnage give texture; they do not yet give human experience.

### Heading 3: “Enforcement must lead to abolition”

**Strongest campaign section.**

Clear distinction between immediate measures and desired political endpoint. Readers know what campaign wants.

Two problems remain:

- Vaccination, registration, inspection have independent public-health value. Their purpose cannot be reduced to dismantling trade.
- Absence of nationwide prohibition does not establish “enforcement vacuums.” Existing veterinary, transport, food-safety, theft laws may apply.

Better distinction:

> “Vaccination and exposure treatment must expand now, regardless of legislative change. Campaign demands stronger enforcement alongside phased abolition.”

### Moral clarity, civic anger, respect

**Moral clarity:** strong.  
**Civic anger:** present, mostly declared rather than earned through reported consequences.  
**Respect:** improved, but collective claims weaken it.

“Communities are not asking outsiders to shame Vietnam” introduces outsider judgment unnecessarily. Show Vietnamese agency through named sources, local reporting, specific testimony. Do not announce national unity.

**Best emotional asset:** preventable exposure plus theft of owned animals. Build there, not through sweeping claims about what Vietnamese people believe.

## 3. Evidentiary Rigor & Causal Integrity

### Sources: useful placement, incomplete accountability

| Evidence | What works | What needs fixing |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuổi Trẻ incident** | Source attached directly to lead claim | Verify attack date versus test-result date; establish incident circumstances |
| **Báo Tây Ninh prosecution** | Names, sentences, counts create checkable specificity | Verify exact convictions, year, appeal status if relevant, meaning of cumulative tonnage |
| **Báo Mới figures** | National burden receives linked attribution | Identify originating outlet or official authority; align year, reporting period, administrative units |
| **Four Paws/local survey** | Potential evidence of domestic opposition | Missing direct citation, named research partner, sample, geography, question wording, denominator |

### Publication-blocking issues

**1. Incident chronology drifts across channels.**

Body says dog tested positive on 7 September after attacking four people. Excerpt and Facebook place attack itself on 7 September. Telegram places attack before positive result that day.

These versions are not equivalent.

**Fix:** establish dated event from source, then lock identical chronology across channels. Confirm intended publication date follows all reported events, including 9 September sentencing.

**2. Household incident does not establish trade involvement.**

“In parallel” helps distinguish subjects. Yet opener, heading, Facebook transition still encourage readers to treat household attack as evidence against trade.

Add explicit boundary:

> “Supplied reporting does not link this household incident to dog-meat trading. Risks during transport, holding, and slaughter require separate evidence.”

Remove qualification only if verified reporting establishes connection.

**3. National totals do not attribute deaths to trade.**

164 outbreaks and 51 deaths establish reported rabies burden. They do not quantify trade’s contribution.

Facebook says:

> “They expose a preventable weakness…”

Figures alone do not expose informal transport failures. Source those failures separately.

**4. Biosecurity claims need direct evidence.**

“Animals move across provincial borders without verified ownership, vaccination proof, quarantine, or veterinary inspection” reads as documented system-wide finding.

Provide sourced investigation or enforcement evidence. Distinguish:

- Documented violations.
- Biologically plausible exposure routes.
- Unknown prevalence.
- Campaign policy judgment.

“These risks … prove” collapses those categories. Replace with scoped, attributed claims.

**5. Survey cannot carry national consensus without methodology.**

“Roughly 95%” lacks usable denominator. Respondents where? Recruited how? Opposed trade, consumption, theft, or another proposition?

“Cited in the research” exposes internal workflow instead of giving readers evidence.

Link original study and state exact finding. If unavailable, remove statistic and national-consensus language.

**6. Public-health evidence does not independently prove necessity of total abolition.**

Campaign may advocate abolition on animal-welfare, theft, governance, and public-health grounds. Supplied evidence does not establish abolition of consumption as medically necessary condition for rabies elimination.

State political demand honestly. Do not disguise policy preference as settled epidemiological conclusion.

### Medical guidance: retain, tighten

Existing advice gets urgency right. “Virtually 100% fatal” after clinical onset is sound.

Use standard, sourced safety wording:

> **After a potentially rabies-exposing bite or scratch, immediately wash and flush the wound with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes and seek urgent medical care. Saliva contacting broken skin, eyes, or mouth also requires urgent assessment. Do not wait for symptoms. A clinician should assess the need for rabies vaccination and rabies immunoglobulin.**

This is safety information, not campaign ornament. Cite WHO or appropriate national health authority.

## 4. Multi-Channel Evaluation

### Facebook

**Improvement:** hook first, link last. Native post now offers readable argument rather than outbound-link wrapper.

**Remaining weaknesses:**

- Attack date may be misstated.
- “One incident shows why every animal movement matters” overgeneralizes unlinked household event.
- National statistics do not establish transport failures.
- “Every dog-meat product carries rabies” introduces misleading food-product framing. Main discussed risk concerns exposure to infected animals, saliva, and tissues during handling—not universal foodborne transmission.
- Survey claim becomes broader as methodology disappears.
- “Tools on road” and “Sign petition” sound clipped rather than professionally edited.

**Fix:** keep incident, one burden statistic, theft case, explicit campaign demand. Remove defensive “not every product” sentence. Replace with precise handling-risk statement backed by source.

### Telegram

**Improvement:** scannable, concrete, clear petition link.

**Weakness:** five bullets still resemble miniature dossier. Compression also strips necessary distinctions:

- Survey sample becomes “Vietnamese respondents.”
- “1.6 tonnes involved” leaves period and meaning unclear.
- Different convictions flatten into “for dog theft.”
- Transport violations appear established without source attribution.

**Fix:** three bullets: reported burden, verified court finding, campaign demand. Preserve qualification where evidence requires it. Omit survey until sourced.

### Shared distribution rule

**Social copy must not become more certain than body copy.**

One verified fact sheet should govern all channels: dates, years, names, charges, sentences, counts, source scope, campaign-versus-government targets.
