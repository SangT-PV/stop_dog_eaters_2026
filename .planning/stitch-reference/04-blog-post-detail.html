The Hidden Epidemiological Risks of the Illegal Meat Trade

By Dr. Julian Thorne

Published on October 24, 2024 • 12 min read

The intersection of traditional cultural practices and modern epidemiological standards has long been a flashpoint for international health organizations. However, the clandestine nature of the dog meat trade introduces a variable that standard food safety protocols cannot account for: a total absence of traceability.

In our three-month investigation across Southeast Asia, we documented how these supply chains bypass every established biosafety barrier. Animals are transported across borders in cramped, unsanitary conditions that serve as a perfect crucible for zoonotic pathogen transmission.

"This isn't just an animal welfare crisis; it is a ticking time bomb for the next global pandemic, hidden in plain sight within illegal markets."

Rabies remains the most immediate threat. In regions where the trade is most active, rabies transmission rates correlate significantly with the movement of unregulated dog meat. The lack of vaccination records and the high-stress environment of transport increase viral shedding, putting both handlers and consumers at catastrophic risk.

Furthermore, the use of prohibited antibiotics and chemicals in the processing of the meat introduces long-term toxicity concerns. Without government oversight, these substances enter the food chain, contributing to the growing global crisis of antimicrobial resistance.
